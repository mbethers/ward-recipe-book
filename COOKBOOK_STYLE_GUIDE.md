# University Ward Cookbook — House Style Guide

**Version 1.2** | Last updated: August 2026

> **Changed in 1.2:** inches used as an adjective are hyphenated with no period
> (`9-in dish`).
>
> **Changed in 1.1:** fractions are now written in plain slash form (`1/2`), reversing the
> superscript rule from 1.0. Added the package-size rule (`1 8oz.` → `8 oz.`).

This guide ensures consistent, professional formatting across all recipes in the cookbook. All submissions (typed, photo/PDF, or link-imported) must be reviewed and corrected to match this style before publishing.

---

## Ingredients

### Measurement Abbreviations
Use the following abbreviations **with periods**:
- **Tbsp.** (tablespoon) — capital T, lowercase "bsp.", with period
- **tsp.** (teaspoon) — lowercase, with period
- **oz.** (ounce) — lowercase, with period
- **lb.** (pound) — lowercase, with period
- **cup** (do not abbreviate) — spelled out, no period
- Numbers and whole units: "8", "1/2 cup", "1 Tbsp.", not "1T", "1 tbsp", "1 T.", "1 Tbsp"

**The period is not optional.** Every abbreviated unit carries one — `Tbsp.`, `tsp.`,
`oz.`, `lb.` Only `cup`, which is spelled out in full, has no period.

### Fractions
Use **plain slash fractions**. Do **not** use the superscript/vulgar fraction characters:
- Write as: `1/4 cup`, `1/2 tsp.`, `3/4 cup`, `1/3 cup`
- ✗ Not: `¼ cup`, `½ tsp.`, `¾ cup`, `⅓ cup`
- Mixed numbers get a space between the whole number and the fraction: `1 1/2 cups`, not `1½ cups`

Slash form is typed the same way on every device and keyboard, so submissions and the
published book stay consistent without anyone hunting for a special character.

### Ingredient Line Structure
Follow this exact order:

**`number unit. [descriptor] ingredient [prep]`**

Examples:
- `1 Tbsp. avocado oil` (simple ingredient)
- `3 Tbsp. melted butter` (prep at the end)
- `2 cups coarsely crushed pretzels` (descriptor before ingredient)
- `1/2 medium onion, chopped` (descriptor before ingredient, prep after)
- `4 cloves garlic, minced` (unit is part of measurement: "cloves")
- `16 oz. super firm tofu` (when specifying a product type, include in descriptor)
- `1/2 cup cooking oil (or substitute w/ applesauce)` (clarifications in parentheses are fine)

### Dimensions Used as Adjectives
When a measurement in inches describes the thing that follows it, hyphenate it and
abbreviate to `in` — **no period**, because the hyphen already binds it to the noun and a
period mid-phrase reads like a sentence ending:

- ✓ `9-in pie crust`, `9-in dish`, `8-in square pan`, `1-in cubes`
- ✗ `9in dish`, `9 in dish`, `9 in. dish`, `9 inch dish`, `9-inch dish`

If the measurement is **not** describing a following noun, it isn't an adjective, so leave
it unhyphenated and spell the unit out:

- ✓ `Roll the dough out to about 9 inches.`
- ✗ `Roll the dough out to about 9-in.`

**Pan dimensions written as a pair keep their conventional form** — `9x13 pan`, not
`9-in x 13-in pan`.

### Package & Container Sizes
Drop a redundant leading `1` in front of a package size, and put a space between the
number and its unit:

- ✓ `8 oz. cream cheese`
- ✗ `1 8oz. cream cheese`

**This applies only when the leading count is exactly 1.** If two or more packages are
called for, the count carries real information, so leave the line exactly as written:

- `3 8oz. cans tomato sauce` — leave as-is, do not rewrite
- `2 15oz. cans black beans` — leave as-is, do not rewrite

**Rules:**
- Start each ingredient with lowercase (unless a proper noun: "Skippy peanut butter", "Italian herbs", "Greek yogurt")
- Use commas to separate prep instructions from the main ingredient: "butter, melted" NOT "melted butter" unless "melted" is part of the product name ("melted chocolate chips" is the product)
- Descriptors that modify the ingredient (color, texture, preparation) go after the unit but before the ingredient name
- Prep instructions (how to prepare it: minced, chopped, melted, beaten, etc.) go after the ingredient name with a comma

### Non-Numeric Measures
Leave as-is with no special formatting:
- "kosher salt to taste"
- "powdered sugar"
- "pepper to taste"
- "fresh cilantro, finely minced"

## Instructions

### Structure
Number each step as a separate line or bullet point:
```
1. Preheat oven to 350°F.
2. Mix flour, sugar, and salt in a bowl.
3. Add eggs one at a time, stirring well.
```

**Rules:**
- Start each step with a capital letter
- End each step with a period
- Be concise but clear
- Use active voice ("Mix the flour" not "The flour should be mixed")
- Include temperatures in Fahrenheit: 350°F, not "350 degrees" or "350 F"

### Substeps & Sections
For recipes with multiple sections (e.g., "For the crust:", "Red Sauce:"), use **bold unindented headers** followed by numbered steps that restart at 1 for each section:

```
**For the crust:**
1. Mix crumbs and butter.
2. Press into pan.

**For the filling:**
1. Whisk eggs until smooth.
2. Add milk and sugar.
```

## Prep Time & Servings

### Prep Time Format
Write as: `30 min`, `1 hr`, `1 hr 15 min`, `2 hrs 30 min`

**Rules:**
- Use lowercase abbreviations: "min" (not "MIN", "Min", "minutes", "m")
- Use "hr" for hours (not "hrs", "hour", "h", "Hr.")
- Space between number and unit: "30 min", not "30min"
- Examples:
  - ✓ `30 min`
  - ✓ `1 hr`
  - ✓ `1 hr 15 min`
  - ✗ `30 minutes`, `1 hour`, `1h 15m`, `30m`

### Servings Format
Write as plain numeric values or ranges:
- `8` (serves 8)
- `8–10` (em dash, not hyphen)
- `makes 2 dozen` (if more natural for the recipe type)
- `48 cookies` (if the recipe naturally yields a countable item)

**Rules:**
- Use em-dash for ranges: `8–10`, not `8-10` or `8 to 10`
- Free text is acceptable if it reads more naturally

## Story / Notes / Memory

No special formatting required. Write naturally and conversationally:
- First-person is fine: "This is Grammy Jo's famous recipe…"
- Parenthetical asides are fine: "…and it's even better with (or without) the nuts."
- Capitalize normally: treat it like a short personal note

## Capitalization Rules

### Recipe Name
Capitalize the first word and proper nouns only:
- ✓ `Belgian Waffles`
- ✓ `Grandma Jo's Fruit Salad`
- ✓ `Low N' Slow Oven-Barbecued Brisket`
- ✗ `Belgian waffles` (first word should be capitalized)
- ✗ `Chocolate Chip Cookies` (both words capitalized unnecessarily)

### Ingredients
- Start with lowercase unless it's a proper noun (brand name, ethnic origin, etc.)
- ✓ `Italian herbs`, `Skippy peanut butter`, `Greek yogurt`, `Dijon mustard`
- ✓ `cornstarch`, `butter`, `flour`, `salt`
- ✗ `Cornstarch`, `Butter`, `Flour` (unless part of a brand name)

### Instructions
- Start each step with a capital letter
- Follow normal English capitalization rules within each step
- Proper nouns stay capitalized: "Preheat oven to 350°F" (F is the proper abbreviation)

## Punctuation

- **Periods on abbreviations**: Always (Tbsp., tsp., oz., lb.)
- **Commas in ingredient lists**: Use commas to separate prep instructions: "butter, melted"
- **Em-dashes in ranges**: Use em-dash (–) not hyphen (-): "8–10 servings"
- **Parentheses**: Fine for clarifications: "(or substitute w/ applesauce)"

---

## Special Characters

- **Fractions**: Plain slash form (1/4, 1/2, 3/4) — never the superscript characters (¼, ½, ¾)
- **Temperature**: Use degree symbol (°F), not "degrees" or "F". Temperatures are **always
  Fahrenheit** — never convert to Celsius, and never assume a bare number is Celsius
- **Em-dashes**: Use for ranges (–) not hyphens (-)
- **Apostrophes**: Straight quotes in names: "Grandma Jo's", not "Grandma Jo's"

## Admin Review Checklist

When reviewing submissions before publishing, check:

- [ ] **Measurements**: All Tbsp., tsp., oz., lb. have periods; cups are spelled out
- [ ] **Fractions**: Slash form (1/4, 1/2) used; no superscript characters (¼, ½)
- [ ] **Package sizes**: A leading `1` is dropped (`8 oz.`, not `1 8oz.`); counts of 2+ left as written
- [ ] **Inches**: Hyphenated with no period when adjectival (`9-in dish`); spelled out otherwise (`9 inches`)
- [ ] **Ingredient line order**: number unit. [descriptor] ingredient [prep]
- [ ] **Capitalization**: Recipe name proper case; ingredients lowercase unless proper noun; steps start with capital
- [ ] **Instructions**: Numbered, each starts with capital, each ends with period
- [ ] **Prep time**: Format is "30 min" or "1 hr 15 min"
- [ ] **Servings**: Numeric or "makes X"; ranges use em-dash (–)
- [ ] **No typos**: Run through proofreading (already happens automatically, but double-check)
- [ ] **Descriptor placement**: "melted butter" not "butter melted"; "avocado oil" not "oil, avocado"
